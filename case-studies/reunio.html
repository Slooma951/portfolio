Reunio

A lost-property app for college and venue front desks. Keep found items organised and help staff reunite them with their owners.

The problem

A found item can sit at a desk while its owner asks somewhere else. Paper notes and scattered messages make it harder to connect the two. Reunio brings item records and lost reports into one place.

Why this matters now

Nearly 19,000 items reached Dublin Airport lost property in 2024; 56% were returned to their owners. That leaves about 44%, over 8,000 items, not returned. The latter figures are approximate calculations from the reported totals. daa, reported by The Irish Post, December 2024.

Transport for London receives about 6,000 lost items each week, and fewer than one in five is reclaimed. After three months, unclaimed items go to charity or auction. TfL figures reported by Eastern Eye. TfL, lost-property disposal.

Dublin Bus, Irish Rail and Luas keep lost property for a maximum of 30 days. Transport for Ireland. Transport for Ireland, holding period.

In May 2026, a Dublin college student hub told students it held over 100 items, all recorded on a spreadsheet. Source: the student hub's email to students, supplied by the project author. The college and private email are not published here.

The problem Reunio addresses is often a missed connection: an item is already there, but its owner asks at the wrong desk or on the wrong day. A notebook or spreadsheet holds the record, and the holding period can end before the owner finds it.

Reunio gives each found item a tag number and a record in about 20 seconds; a photo can help fill in what it is. Lost reports are matched against the shelf with reasons shown. Staff check ownership before a return, and the desk can track items logged, items returned and time to return.

No desk has used Reunio yet, so it has no measured return rate. A free pilot is how that would be measured. There is no evidence yet that Reunio raises return rates.

How it works

  1. Log a found item. Record its kind, colour, location and useful details. Give it a reference for its tag.
  2. Take a lost report. Capture what the owner remembers and review suggested matches with the reasons behind them.
  3. Check before returning. Staff confirm ownership and record the handover. A suggested match does not release an item automatically.

What I built

A React Native and Expo app with a searchable shelf, item logging, lost reports and desk settings. Item histories and return steps help staff follow what happened. The web preview supports local image suggestions for logging, with manual entry available when a suggestion is uncertain.

Engineering choices

The interface gives staff a short path from logging to review. Matching explanations keep the decision understandable. Records are kept on the device in this prototype, and retention controls help a desk manage stored information.

Current stage

Reunio is a working prototype. It has not yet been used in a live front-desk pilot. The screenshots use example data, and real-world reliability, shared-desk operation and deployment still need evaluation.

Why it matters

For students, losing keys, a phone or a bag can interrupt a whole day. A clearer record gives front-desk staff a practical way to help, while keeping ownership checks part of the return.
